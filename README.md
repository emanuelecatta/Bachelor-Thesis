# Bachelor-Thesis
Bachelor thesis: Gauge invariance and the string action, done in academic year 2024/25.

INTRO
String theories represent one of the most current attempts to unify the four fundamental interactions
in a single system [1]: the electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions, formalised by quantum field
theories, together with gravity, which we describe through general relativity, a classical field theory.
The first goal, still unresolved, which these theories try at least in part to clarify, is to understand
how to build a theory of Quantum gravity, able to explain the quantum structure of spacetime, which
could then be joined to the other Quantum field theories (QFT).
String theories begin by describing the interactions through fundamental 1D objects, the strings,
whose low-energy vibration modes reproduce the behaviour of the particles in the traditional QFTs
used for the standard model. In particular, from the point of view of their mathematical structure
they turn out to be QFTs in a 2D space, the world-sheet of the string.
A property shared by these theories is that they are gauge theories and that, besides invariances
common to the known field theories, they also exhibit others specific to 2D QFTs, such as Weyl
conformal invariance. Today the study of these invariances, in particular following the AdS/CFT
correspondence conjecture [2], enjoys great success and interest across the various lines of research.
The aim of this thesis is to introduce the reader to some of the elementary features of string theories,
by studying the closed Polyakov string in flat spacetime.
In the first chapter we will introduce the classical Nambu-Goto and Polyakov strings and their formulations
in the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms, and we will identify the symmetries and gauge
invariances of the system.
In the second chapter we will discuss what it means to have gauge invariances and to decide to eliminate
them from a Hamiltonian and symplectic point of view, introducing some properties of constrained
Hamiltonian systems; we will then put what we discussed into practice on our system by applying the
uniform light-cone gauge [3], and using the constraint conditions to compute the metric perturbatively.
Finally, in the third chapter we will first study the reduced system through a perturbative approach,
following what was done in [3]: we will compute, in the decompactification limit, the Hamiltonian up
to quartic order in the fields, the unperturbed solutions of the motion, the dispersion law for the free
string and the tree-level Scattering matrix (generalising the result to the case of relativistic effective
actions). Then, recalling results from integrability theory, we will discuss the computation of the
spectrum of the Hamiltonian in finite volume, with a mention of the corrections and of the neglected 
contributions. This result will show that, although the theory seems to depend on a gauge parameter,
the physical observables, in particular the spectrum, are in fact independent of the gauge choice, as
we expect it should be.
Although the treatment is elementary and adopts a simple and well-known model for the analysis, it
is enough to modify the ingredients only slightly to turn the questions analysed into topics of current
research interest. For example, in superstring theory the action used is made of a fermionic term
(itself subject to gauge-invariance issues) and of a bosonic one, described by an action analogous
to Polyakov’s. These superstrings can be placed in curved spacetimes, usually compatible with the
application of the AdS/CFT correspondence, as done for AdS5×T5 in [3] or for AdS3×S3×T4 in [11]
or in [4]. In these cases the computation of the Scattering matrix and of the Hamiltonian spectrum
requires bootstrap techniques that exploit properties related to the integrability of these models, and
in several cases it is still an open problem.
Finally, the Nambu-Goto string turns out to be non-trivial enough to be used in the study of theories
of Quantum gravity [5], and it allows one to analyse within the same theory phenomena such as Quantum Black Holes and the Heisenberg uncertainty principle for strings.
